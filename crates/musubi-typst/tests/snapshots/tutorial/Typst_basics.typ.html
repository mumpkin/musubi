Typst basics

A note is a Typst file. Most of it is plain text with a little markup. Press i to see how this note is written: the source is the best example.

Headings

The first line with one = is the title of the note; it also names its file. == starts a section, === a subsection.

Text

  • *strong* gives strong, _emphasis_ gives emphasis.
  • Backticks give inline code.
  • An empty line starts a new paragraph; a \ at the end of a line breaks it.
  • Web links: #link("https://typst.app")[Typst] gives Typst, which opens in your browser.
  • A footnote: #footnote[…]1.
  • Colors and sizes: #text(fill: blue)[…] gives blue text, #text(size: 14pt)[…] larger text.

  • A line starting with - is a bullet.

Term
A line starting with / and ending the term with : describes a term.

Going further

Everything else Typst can do works too: functions, variables, shapes, grids. What the reading view cannot show as text is drawn as an image. The Typst tutorial explains it all.

Five short pages show what that gives, each with comments in its source (press i), in Welcome/Advanced Examples/: a character sheet, Cornell notes, a revision sheet, a journal page and a drawn map.

Packages from Typst Universe (#import "@preview/…") are not available: Musubi works offline and downloads nothing. A note that imports one still shows, with a notice where the import is and in place of what uses the package, followed by its source.
