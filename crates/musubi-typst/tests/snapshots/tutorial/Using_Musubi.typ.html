Using Musubi

A guide to the whole app. Come back to it when you look for something; the command palette (Space ?) lists the same commands.

The window

  • Title bar: the title of the current note, and the buttons of the window.
  • Explorer, on the left (Space e): folders and notes by title. Next to the vault name, the flashcards due, and a menu (…) to edit the tree as text, create a note or a folder, and review. A right click on a note or a folder, or Shift-F10 on the entry under the keyboard, opens its menu: open, pin, new note or folder in it, rename or move, archive, trash. Drag a note or a folder onto a folder (or onto Notebook, for the top) to move it there; links follow. Long titles end with “…”: the tooltip shows the full path. Three sections fold like folders: Pinned, the notes you pinned; Recent, the four notes modified last; Notebook, all your folders and notes. Below come Archive and Trash, when they are not empty; the settings can hide the trash.
  • Panes, in the middle: one, or two side by side or one above the other. Each pane shows a note, read or edited, or the graph. Without a note (a note moved to the trash, Space o), a pane shows the home screen: find a note, the notes modified last, the pinned ones, the flashcards due. Drag the line between panes to resize them.
  • Backlinks, on the right (Space b): the notes that link to the current one.
  • Space h and Space l move the keyboard to the panel on the left and on the right, folded panels skipped. In the explorer and the backlinks, j and k move, Enter or l opens a note or folds a folder, Escape goes back to the pane. A note opened from the backlinks takes the keyboard: j and k move in it at once.
  • The header of each pane shows its mode: READ; NORMAL, INSERT, SELECT in the editor; GRAPH. The panel with the keyboard (explorer, pane, backlinks) has its header highlighted. Messages (saved, errors) show for a few seconds at the bottom right.

Click a link to open its note. Footnote numbers and the entries of an outline scroll to their place. Web links, marked with a small external link icon like the Typst website, open in your browser.

Editor

The editor follows Helix; Helix basics explains its keys. The commands of the command line (:):

CommandAction
:wSave
:qBack to reading; refuses unsaved changes
:q!Back to reading, dropping the changes
:wq, :xSave, then back to reading

Leaving a note with unsaved changes (another note opened in the pane, the pane closed, another vault) asks first: s saves, d discards, Escape stays on the note.

In insert mode, [[ links to a note, Ctrl-v pastes an image. Dropping an image on the editor inserts it too.

Notes

  • Create: Space n, the menu (…) of the explorer, or the menu of a folder. The file is named from the title. Type a path to choose the folder: Projects/Ideas/My note creates My note in Projects/Ideas, and the missing folders with it.
  • Rename: change the first = heading and save. The file, its images and every link to it follow.
  • Pin: the menu of the note in the explorer, or “Pin or unpin the note” in the palette. Pins are saved in the vault: every copy of it shows them. A renamed note stays pinned; a note moved to the trash is unpinned.
  • Archive: the menu of a note or a folder in the explorer, or “Archive or unarchive the note” in the palette. It moves to the Archive folder, shown in the Archive section, and links to it follow; the same menu puts it back.
  • Delete: the menu of the note in the explorer, or “Move the note to the trash” in the palette. Folders go to the trash the same way, with everything in them. “Show the trash”, or the Trash section of the explorer (a click restores an entry, its menu deletes it for good), restores notes and folders or deletes them for good. The links to a note in the trash become their plain text; restoring the note brings them back, unless you edited that text meanwhile. Deleted for good, the note is forgotten: links to it are to be made again by hand. Notes stay 30 days in the trash; the settings change the delay.
  • Changes made elsewhere (another program, a sync tool) show when you come back to the window.

Edit the tree as text

Space r in the explorer, or Edit the tree as text in its menu (…), turns it into an editor holding every folder and note of the vault, one per line, as in the oil.nvim plugin of Neovim. Folders end with /; what a folder holds is indented under it. Edit it with the Helix keys, then :w:

  • change a title to rename the note, a folder name to rename the folder;
  • cut a line (x then d) and paste it (p) under another folder to move it, or change its indentation with > and <;
  • add a line to create a note, or a folder if it ends with /; Projects/Ideas/ creates both folders;
  • delete a line to move the note or the folder to the trash.

Before anything changes, a window lists what will happen: y applies it, n goes back to the text. Links follow renamed and moved notes. A line that cannot work is underlined, with the reason, and nothing changes. Enter opens the note of the line; :q goes back to the explorer, :q! drops the changes.

Settings and themes

Space , opens the settings; every change applies at once.

  • Vault: the open vault, the vaults opened before on this computer (a click opens one), and “Open a folder…” for another; an empty folder becomes a new vault, with this tutorial. Save your changes first.
  • Theme: Musubi follows the light or dark preference of the system, with the colors of Ayu Light and Ayu Mirage, or uses the theme you choose (GitHub Light and GitHub Dark are there too). “Import a theme” adds one from a JSON file; the guide docs/themes.md of the repository describes the format. The palette has the same commands: type theme in it.
  • Editor: save automatically after a few seconds without typing; relative line numbers, as in Helix: the other lines show their distance to the cursor, handy for 5j.
  • Flashcards: how many new cards a review brings each day (20 by default).
  • Trash of this vault: how many days deleted notes stay in the trash (0 keeps them).

The theme and autosave are settings of this computer; the trash delay belongs to the vault, so every copy of it uses the same.

Where your notes live

Your vault is an ordinary folder; Musubi only reads and writes files in it.

  • Each note is a .typ file, readable and compilable with Typst itself.
  • The images of a note are in a folder next to it, Note.assets/.
  • .musubi/ holds the settings of the vault, its pinned notes and the trash.
  • The search index and the window layout stay on this computer, outside the vault: deleting them loses nothing.

Musubi has no sync of its own. To use your notes on several devices, sync the folder with the tool you like: Syncthing, Nextcloud, git…

When a note changed on two devices at once, the sync tool keeps a copy of the other version next to it. Musubi lists these copies in a Conflicts section of the explorer, and marks the note in its header: open one to see both versions side by side, the differences marked, then keep this version (1), the other one (2), or merge by hand (m). The version you drop goes to the trash.
